How Fuel Cost is Calculated

Calculating the fuel needed for a journey involves three variables: total journey distance, vehicle fuel consumption rate, and local fuel price per unit.

Fuel Required = Distance ÷ Fuel Economy (for km/L or MPG)
Fuel Required = (Distance ÷ 100) × Fuel Economy (for L/100km)
Total Cost = Fuel Required × Price per Fuel Unit

Differences in Fuel Economy Units

  • km/L (Kilometers per Liter): Common in Asia, South America, and Europe. Higher is better (e.g., 18 km/L is more fuel-efficient than 12 km/L).
  • L/100km (Liters per 100 km): Standard metric unit in Canada, Europe, and Australia. Lower is better (e.g., 6.0 L/100km consumes less fuel than 9.5 L/100km).
  • MPG (Miles per Gallon): Standard in the United States and United Kingdom. Note that a UK Imperial gallon (4.546 L) is 20% larger than a US liquid gallon (3.785 L).
