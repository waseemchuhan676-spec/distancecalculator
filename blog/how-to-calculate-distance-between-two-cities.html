How to Calculate Distance Between Two Cities

Published on October 28, 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026 · By Navigation & Geodesy Editorial Team · 6 min read

Whether you are planning a weekend road trip, moving to a new state, estimating business travel reimbursement, or organizing freight logistics, knowing the exact distance between two cities is essential.

However, many travelers are surprised to find that different tools give conflicting numbers. One website might say that two cities are 340 kilometers apart, while another claims 415 kilometers. The reason lies in the distinction between road driving distance and straight-line ("as the crow flies") distance.

Step 1: Understand the Two Types of City Distance

Before entering city names into a search engine, clarify which type of measurement your situation requires:

  • Driving Distance (Road Network): The actual vehicular mileage traveled along public highways, turnpikes, and municipal roadways. This is the number you need for calculating gas consumption, road tolls, driving time, and vehicle wear-and-tear.
  • Straight-Line Distance (Great-Circle): The direct geodesic distance across the curved surface of the Earth between two points. This measurement is used in aviation flight planning, telecommunications, radio line-of-sight, and regional zoning.
💡 Rule of Thumb: Driving road distance is usually 15% to 40% longer than the straight-line distance — about 16% longer on average across 13 popular US routes, ranging from 7% to 48%.

The Haversine Formula (Step by Step)

For the straight-line distance, geographers use the Haversine formula, which finds the great-circle distance between two points on a sphere from their latitudes and longitudes:

a = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat₁) · cos(lat₂) · sin²(Δlon/2)
c = 2 · atan2(√a, √(1−a))
d = R · c, where R = 6,371 km (3,959 miles)

Worked example — New York (40.7128, −74.0060) to Los Angeles (34.0522, −118.2437): convert the latitude and longitude differences to radians, then a = 0.092411, c = 0.617760 radians, and d = 6,371 × 0.617760 = 3,935.75 km (2,446 miles), on a bearing of 273.7°. At a cruising speed of 900 km/h that is about 4 hours 22 minutes in the air. The road route is longer — about 4,500 km — so the same trip by car takes far longer.

The formula treats the Earth as a perfect sphere, so on long routes it differs from the WGS84 ellipsoid figure by up to about 0.5% — New York to Los Angeles is 2,451 miles on the ellipsoid, 5 miles more than the Haversine figure.

Step 3: Worked Example (London to Edinburgh)

Let's look at a concrete real-world example between London, the capital of the United Kingdom, and Edinburgh, the capital of Scotland.

  • Straight-Line Distance (Haversine): 534 km (332 miles) heading North-Northwest (bearing 339°).
  • Fastest Driving Route: Approximately 650 km (404 miles).
  • Driving Difference: The road route is 116 km (21.7%) longer than the straight-line distance.

How to Measure Distance in Google Maps

You can measure the straight-line distance between any two points in Google Maps. On a computer, right-click the starting point, choose "Measure distance", then click to add points — the total shows at the bottom, and you can drag points to move them or click one to remove it. On a phone, touch and hold to drop a pin, tap the pin name, then "Measure distance" and move the map to add points. Note that Measure distance gives the straight-line distance only — for driving miles, use Directions instead.

Common Mistakes When Calculating City Distance

To ensure your travel calculations are reliable, watch out for these frequent oversights:

  • Ambiguous City Names: There are dozens of cities named "Springfield" or "Richmond" in the United States alone. Always confirm the state or country when selecting your origin or destination.
  • Ignoring City Center Offsets: Most mapping platforms calculate distance from the historic center (e.g., City Hall or Central Post Office). If your actual start point is on the suburban perimeter, your true trip distance could differ by 20–30 km.
  • Forgetting Return Mileage: A common budgeting blunder is calculating fuel expenses for a one-way trip when planning a round-trip vacation. Use our Fuel Cost Calculator with the Round-trip toggle enabled.
  • Confusing Statute Miles with Nautical Miles: 1 statute mile equals 1.609 km, while 1 nautical mile equals 1.852 km. Mixing these up introduces a 15% calculation error.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is driving distance between two cities longer than straight-line distance?

Driving distance is usually 15% to 40% longer than the straight-line distance, because roads follow the road network and must go around mountains, water and towns — about 16% longer on average across 13 popular US routes.

How can I calculate the straight-line distance between two cities?

Use the Haversine formula with the latitude and longitude of each city: a = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat₁)·cos(lat₂)·sin²(Δlon/2); c = 2·atan2(√a, √(1−a)); distance = 6,371 km × c. Or use our calculator above, or Google Maps' Measure distance tool.

How do I calculate driving time once I have the distance?

Divide the distance by your anticipated average speed. For instance, a 300 km highway journey at 100 km/h takes roughly 3 hours of driving. You can use our Travel Time Calculator to include rest stops and generate arrival ETAs.

How accurate is the Haversine formula?

Very accurate for most trips: the spherical-Earth Haversine result differs from the WGS84 ellipsoid figure by up to about 0.5% on long routes — for example, New York to Los Angeles is 2,446 miles by Haversine vs 2,451 miles on the ellipsoid, a 5-mile difference.

How do I measure distance in Google Maps?

On a computer, right-click the starting point, choose 'Measure distance', then click to add points — the total shows at the bottom. On a phone, touch and hold to drop a pin, tap the pin name, then 'Measure distance'. It measures straight-line distance only; use Directions for driving miles.

Why do two tools give different distances for the same cities?

Distances are measured centre-to-centre, so a specific address can differ noticeably, and airports sit 10–30 miles outside city centres. Tools also use different routing engines, and road figures drift as routes change — results differing by more than a few miles usually mean a different start or end point was used.

How do I convert kilometres to miles?

Multiply kilometres by 0.621371 to get miles (1 mile = 1.609344 km). For nautical miles, 1 nautical mile = 1.852 km = 1.15078 statute miles.
