How to Estimate Driving Time Between Cities

Published on October 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Accurately estimating driving time is critical when organizing holiday road trips, coordinating business meetings, or scheduling delivery shipments. Yet drivers regularly arrive later than expected because they base their timeline on highway speed limits rather than their true average velocity.

The Fundamental Speed-Distance-Time Formula

At its mathematical foundation, travel time is determined by dividing distance by velocity:

Time (Hours) = Distance ÷ Average Speed
Total Elapsed Travel Time = Moving Time + Rest Breaks

For example, if you are driving a distance of 350 kilometers (217 miles) and maintain an average speed of 85 km/h (53 mph):

  • Pure Moving Time = 350 km ÷ 85 km/h = 4.12 hours (4 hours, 7 minutes).
  • If you schedule a 30-minute lunch stop and a 15-minute fuel break, your total journey duration will be 4 hours and 52 minutes.

Why Highway Speed Limits Do Not Equal Average Speed

A common error is assuming that if the posted highway speed limit is 70 mph (110 km/h), your average speed for the entire trip will be 70 mph.

In reality, your true trip average speed is virtually always 10 to 20 mph lower than the posted speed limit due to:

  • Exiting the highway through signalized intersections and suburban arterials.
  • Toll plaza slowdowns and merge lanes.
  • Passing slower trucks or construction work zones.
  • Heavy traffic density around metropolitan bypasses.
⏱️ Planning Rule: For mixed highway and city trips, plan around an effective average speed of 50–55 mph (80–90 km/h) rather than full maximum highway speeds.

Factoring in Physiological Rest Stops

Driver fatigue is one of the leading causes of highway accidents. Road safety organizations (such as the UK AA and US NHTSA) recommend taking a 15-minute break every two hours of driving.

On an 8-hour drive, scheduled rest stops will naturally add 45 minutes to 1 hour to your arrival time. Factor these into your departure schedule so you don't feel pressured to speed.
